# Visual Authoring

You SHALL produce the visual contribution the actual request needs, with faithful meaning and usable delivery for its audience. The representation, composition and amount of explanation follow the assignment. A diagram, chart, illustration, map, interactive exploration, mockup or something else entirely (you SHALL NOT limit yourself creatively to any pre-defined taxonomy; all things are allowed and you SHOULD explore pre-defined as well as undefined ideas that have not yet been mentioned to find the best visual(s) for the situation) can be appropriate; these examples do not close the available forms. Preserve informed format and style choices while exercising the design judgment entrusted to you.

You SHOULD establish the question, relevant context and evidence relationships before letting controls or decorative elements dominate the reader's entry. Choose visual structure that helps the audience inspect, understand, decide or explore. A narrow requested edit can remain narrow. Where choosing the contribution is delegated, an existing view, ordinary narrative or no additional artifact can be the useful result.

You SHALL preserve consequential identity, units, relationships, qualifications, uncertainty and missingness. Distinguish observations, expectations, hypotheses and illustrative inventions wherever conflation would mislead. Layout, ordering, aggregation, animation and color must not manufacture an evidential weight or conclusion the sources do not support. Use accessible distinctions beyond color where readers depend on them.

Use the actual environment and choose suitable formats and tools. SVG and standalone HTML can be useful artifacts; neither is required for every request. Use appropriate plotting tools when precision or scientific export matters. Native Mermaid source may suffice for a diagram; the adjacent [Mermaid skill](../mermaid/SKILL.md) supplies format-specific guidance when needed. This plugin bundles no report framework, renderer or model service.

When interaction helps, implement the behavior the controls promise and retain enough context to interpret changes. A hypothetical scenario should remain distinguishable from observed data. A mockup's appearance does not establish a working service. Preserve relevant state, selection and evidence when the reader changes views; do not make the interface obscure the question it serves.

You SHOULD adapt layout, typography and detail to the intended consumer and supplied material. There is no universal canvas width, node count, font roster, diagram family or style. Resolve actual clipping and ambiguity without hiding labels, changing evidence, or replacing legitimate variation with an example's incidental choices. Useful defaults remain revisable.

You SHALL check the consequential properties of the delivered interface with the evidence available. Parsing, native rendering, interaction, accessibility and substantive fidelity establish different properties. Use an actual receiving environment where a claim depends on its behavior. Keep an unavailable check explicit and preserve usable work without claiming the property passed. Do not broaden or repeat verification after the required checks pass unless a changed input or material concern warrants it.

A view can be working material, a final explanation, or a handoff. [Sources and visual handoffs](../../references/sources-and-handoffs.md) covers faithful reuse and annotation when those relationships matter. You MAY revise, replace or remove a view as understanding changes; its categories do not define the limits of the inquiry.
