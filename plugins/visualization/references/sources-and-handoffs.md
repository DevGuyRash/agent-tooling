# Sources and visual handoffs

A diagram is a selective view with a purpose and a source history. It can expose a dependency, recurring operation, missing consumer or disagreement without becoming a complete account of reality. Keep original material or reliable access when an independent interpretation could change the work. Source access and the freedom to contribute a different interpretation are separate.

Retain the relevant diagram revision when later observations or annotations refer to it. A stable node, relationship or source location can make a comment recoverable; an omitted object may need a new description rather than an attachment to the nearest existing node. Changed or ambiguous targets should remain unresolved instead of silently moving to another object.

A mark can identify a blocked transition, disputed premise, missing information, or observed failure. Preserve which meaning is intended and its grounds. A reviewer may add a missing relationship, redraw the arrangement or conclude that it is sound. An X, fixed annotation format or mandatory disagreement is not required.

For substantive independent assessment, supply the original assignment and relevant evidence rather than only an author's chosen picture. A diagram-only handoff can be a legitimate test of that actual delivery boundary; its usability does not validate unseen premises. Preserve completed effects, current state and useful source access so reconstruction does not repeat external actions.

An early sketch may later carry observations and then support a simpler final explanation. Reuse is useful when meaning and revision relationships survive. A change in appearance, the number of diagrams, or a more elaborate explanation does not by itself establish improved judgment or lower total effort.
