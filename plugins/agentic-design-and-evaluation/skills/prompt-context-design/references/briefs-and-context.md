# Briefs and context

## Briefs for delegated agents

A brief carries what the recipient cannot know or decide on its own: the original request and materials in their own words (the user's observations, logs, the files involved), the outcome it owns as a state someone can check, the authority it has and the actions it must not take, and where its result goes. Your own diagnosis, decomposition, or preferred fix is a hypothesis: mark it as yours, or leave it out when the recipient's independent reading is the point, as with a reviewer. A handoff supplies information and state, not understanding; the recipient reconstructs understanding from the originals, and completed effects are listed so it does not repeat them.

Before claiming a recipient is fresh, blind, or isolated, inspect what the host actually gives it: inherited instructions and conversation, mounted files, tools, permissions, and shared state.

## Continuing and unattended work

A prompt for work that continues without the user (a successor brief, a compaction handoff, a recurring or scheduled prompt) names the observable state at which the work is finished, what the agent does when it cannot reach that state (report the blocker and stop), and which actions stay with the user. "Until the remaining work is complete" is not an end: an agent can always find remaining work. The user's own words set the scope; plans and notes written by agents record intentions and progress, and a brief keeps the two apart.

## What the recipient actually receives

Context is what reaches the executor, not what exists: a listed resource can be unreadable, a file read can be truncated, and a retained transcript can be absent from active context. Put what every activation needs in the entry the recipient always loads, and conditional detail behind a condition it can resolve from where it is. A skill catalog costs tokens on every task, and hosts shorten or drop descriptions when catalogs grow, so a description names the capability and the situations it fits and nothing more.

An artifact you generate for another executor carries what that executor needs: the meaning, constraints, originals or reliable access, and resources, never a reference to your private notes, the authoring conversation, or a local cache it cannot reach. A delivered snapshot does not change when its source later does.
