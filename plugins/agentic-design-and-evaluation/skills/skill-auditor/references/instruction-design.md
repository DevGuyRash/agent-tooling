# Instruction Design

You SHOULD inspect the instruction as a delegation within a real arrangement. What contribution is entrusted to the executor, which choices are fixed, what original information is available, and what makes completion useful? A target can be flawed because it solves an unnecessary problem, invents authority, or gives the executor the wrong assignment even when its prose and files are consistent.

You SHOULD assess the allocation of work between the executor and its mechanisms. Examine whether a tool is useful, which decisions it fixes, and whether those decisions depend on contextual judgment. Retain mechanisms that serve a defined operation or an established requirement; return context-dependent interpretation and choices to the appropriate executor. Consider setup, context, operational and maintenance costs when retaining, simplifying or removing support.

For an external target, you SHALL use its actual requirements and observed consequences.

Examine whether proposed methods have acquired undeserved authority. A suggested output shape, author-chosen probe, or previous assistant interpretation can become a binding requirement through a follow-up or acceptance test. Conversely, explicit structure may carry a real consumer interface, a hazardous ordering constraint, or useful shared meaning. Neither procedural form nor declarative form proves quality.

You SHOULD look at the work an instruction causes as well as its text. A local workflow can finish while the user's result remains incomplete; an added review or process can redirect a narrow assignment. Trace the actual mandate and steering before attributing excess work: an explicit user expansion is different from agent drift. Apply the same distinction to execution plans, where proposed mechanisms can acquire binding force without a corresponding user requirement, delegated decision, or justified contribution.

For wording that burdens or misdirects the recipient, consult [instruction editing](../../prompt-context-design/references/designing-instructions.md). Ground the finding in the target's actual task, reader, and requirements.

Tests can preserve the wrong property. Assertions about a required sentence, heading roster, prohibited word, or absence of scripts may protect the author's design rather than the consumer's needs. You SHALL keep checks that enforce a real interface or observed failure. Removing an incidental assertion does not require a replacement ceremony; determine which actual property still needs evidence.

You SHOULD evaluate whether instructions leave correction practical. An executor may need to report a defect without owning a complete alternative. It may also need to preserve a justified commitment instead of endlessly reframing. A supported repair changes the earliest actionable cause within scope and checks the relevant effect. Better-looking prose and an executor's claim to have followed it do not establish a behavioral improvement.

Model-specific support needs bounded evidence. A past failure can justify a targeted reminder or test under similar conditions without becoming a universal runtime obligation. You SHALL retain the recipient, task, host, observed effect, and reason for revisiting such support. Many recorded failures have plausible provider, tool, context, or mandate explanations; a history alone does not isolate the skill's causal contribution.

You SHOULD examine whether accumulated support still contributes for the actual recipient. Repeated preparation, generic testing reminders, small isolated increments, and overlapping skill triggers can produce an incomplete consumer workflow even when each local instruction appears reasonable. Trace observed work and authority before diagnosing the cause; a broad user expansion differs from drift. Retaining or removing a support needs appropriate grounds, and a stronger future executor need not reenact the author's decomposition.

A diagram can expose an instruction interaction, lost consumer, recurring loop, or changed mandate. Use or revise that view when it helps, retaining access to original instructions and outcomes. The diagram need not enumerate every concern, and a reviewer can identify what it omitted. A source-grounded direct judgment or no-change result can finish a narrow audit.
