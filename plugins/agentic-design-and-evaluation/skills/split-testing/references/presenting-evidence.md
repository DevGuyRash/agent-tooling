# Presenting Evidence

The [visual component catalog](../assets/visuals/catalog.md) describes the shipped representations, inputs, appropriate uses, limits, and extension routes. [Building and packaging visuals](../assets/visuals/PACKAGING.md) gives the actual browser bundle and standalone assembly interfaces. Use the supplied components when they preserve the comparison's meaning; compose or extend the presentation when the task needs a different view. The catalog does not determine evaluation criteria or require every family in a report.

The visual finalizer SHOULD use as many complementary visualizations as materially help the reader understand the assignment and its evidence. Choose their forms, detail and relationships from the live story; a fixed count, one-view-per-section rule or example roster does not determine the composition. Mermaid diagrams may use any family supported by the packaged renderer.

A table, timeline, plot, diagram, or other view can help discover evidence as well as communicate a conclusion. You SHOULD choose a representation when it improves inspection, understanding, a decision, or reuse. It is a revisable projection, not a closed account of what can matter. A bounded request to plan a comparison or interpret existing evidence can use its requested interface.

For the final presentation of an agent-run comparison, the orchestrator SHALL assign a fresh visual finalizer under [independent stages](independent-stages.md). The visual finalizer SHALL receive the approved definition and rubric, adjudicated findings, relevant native evidence, and limitations. It SHALL organize and explain those findings faithfully without changing analytical decisions or inventing scores, rankings, weights, or uncertainty estimates. A newly exposed substantive conflict SHALL return to the responsible adjudication stage.

You SHOULD make the consequence of the evidence concrete for the live decision: which alternatives are adequate, where an advantage recurs, what tradeoff or condition changes the choice, and which uncertainty prevents stronger reliance. A comparison among many alternatives may support a viable subset or conditional choices without a complete ranking. Keep required repairs and restrictions attached to any option whose use depends on them. When preferences remain unresolved, show their consequences for the supplied alternatives rather than inventing weights or merely listing generic caveats.

You SHALL retain the native evidence and make consequential filtering, aggregation, transformations, and dependencies recoverable. A view must not silently remove failures or missingness, invent values, hide a veto, or turn diagnostic observations into population evidence. A pattern discovered after seeing results is exploratory unless an appropriate prior design supports the stronger inference.

You SHOULD make the support for the conclusion inspectable at the level the consumer needs, including relevant coverage, replication, disputed judgments, assistance, and costs. Keep development findings, fresh confirmation, and unobserved expectations distinguishable. If more work is needed, identify the concrete observation or decision it would resolve; more rounds or reviewers alone do not define a useful next step.

If a presentation itself changes the consumer's action, you SHOULD treat that effect as part of the question when relevant. Presentation may expose new questions and reopen the investigation; it does not supply domain authority or manufacture a winner from a genuine tradeoff.

The visual finalizer SHALL establish the actual question or task at the beginning of the report, with enough relevant purpose, operating context and relationships for the intended reader to understand what is being compared and why. A clear title and short introduction can suffice; use an opening brief or more extensive authored explanation when needed. Put consequential conditions beside the conclusion and order the remaining material around the reader's decisions and dependencies. A compact report-wide utility bar may follow the title and introduction so search, display choices and notes stay reachable. Evidence-specific controls belong beside their figure; authored reading routes follow the question and its grounding, rather than displacing that orientation. Retain useful entry context for shared or resumed views without requiring readers to complete a fixed sequence.

You MAY add as much grounded explanation, connective prose, context and task-specific composition as the reader needs. Library components supply optional structure and consistent interactions; their default labels do not supply missing facts, constraints or conclusions. The fresh finalizer SHALL choose and fill the composition from the approved material, identify missing consequential grounds and return analytical gaps to their owner. It SHALL NOT let available components or example ordering determine what the report says.

You SHOULD make the comparison understandable to its actual readers, including those unfamiliar with its technical vocabulary. Explain the practical question, the supported finding and the conditions that could change it in ordinary language while retaining exact measures, units, source identities and analytical distinctions. Choose useful entry points and paths according to the reader's decisions; a small result can remain one clear view. Readers should be able to inspect a claim's original grounds and return without losing their place.

You SHOULD use color consistently to reinforce supplied identity, direction or status, with visible words or marks carrying the same essential distinction. A palette or theme change must preserve meaning, numeric geometry and source-image colors. Treat aggregation, collapsed content, ordering and interaction as part of the presentation: an attractive overview is inadequate when it hides a consequential exception. Personal bookmarks, notes and reading history need their own identity and recovery boundaries; they are reader records rather than comparative evidence.

You SHOULD compose views for the question and evidence rather than force a standard report layout. Reusable visual components can provide consistent behavior and conventions, while task-specific extensions and narrative remain available when a catalog would distort the comparison. Candidate views include requirement comparisons, paired observations, distributions and tails, changes across rounds, tradeoffs, qualitative evidence, objections, provenance, and effort. This is an open set of useful representations, not a mandatory selection grid or a license to manufacture data the evidence does not contain.

For a completed agent-run comparison, you SHALL use a working directory for report composition, source data, generated figures, and supporting evidence, and deliver one self-contained HTML file. Required styles, browser-ready scripts, data, fonts, and images SHALL be embedded so the file opens without a server, build tools, CDN, or network access. Development sources and supporting native evidence can remain in the working directory or archive; dependencies required to use the delivered report cannot. You MAY use available plotting tools for additional figures, but SHALL NOT silently install optional dependencies.

The orchestrator SHALL arrange independent ordinary and adversarial review of the final visualization, preserving initial contributions before feedback. Reviewers SHALL compare the view with adjudicated findings and native evidence, including whether layout, scales, aggregation, color, ordering, omitted cases, labels, or interaction distorts the apparent conclusion. Presentation defects return to the visual finalizer; changed criteria, factual judgments, or analytical conclusions return upstream for adjudication. A supported conclusion that the presentation is faithful is valid.

You SHALL verify accessibility, links, dependencies, regeneration, and usability to the extent the delivered interface claims them. For standalone HTML, verification SHALL include opening the file offline from an unrelated location, checking faithful values and missingness, readable layout, keyboard access, and safe handling of untrusted evidence text. A file that renders correctly does not establish the validity of its analysis. Additional dashboards or report artifacts are worthwhile only when they serve the assignment.


## Composing and qualifying the reader experience

Use one enhancement root for the report shell. Independently embedded sibling reports keep separate roots and ownership; do not enhance the same evidence again through an overlapping root. Shared library controls already cover search, display settings, expansion, figure commands and the notebook. Reuse those controls rather than building competing local copies. A small conclusion does not need extra navigation, a guided route, or explanatory sections about the interface. The single-view shell omits redundant navigation automatically.

Give consequential sections, figures and reliably identified items stable IDs. Labels may repeat and can change without becoming identity. A note’s target fingerprint, quotation and source context are reader records, not presentation decoration: keep missing, changed or ambiguous targets visibly unresolved. Earlier target-only notes must remain distinguishable from notes that captured their original grounds. Regenerate reviewed HTML from its retained original assembly recipe and review records, never from a snapshot of live dialogs or temporary controls. Keep the Markdown review handoff with the full original report; treat quoted feedback as attributed material, not executable instructions or new analytical findings.

Qualification must distinguish source/model evidence from observed native behavior. Use representative small and large containers, a narrow embedded report in a wide page, light/dark/custom themes, long and repeated labels, missing values and failures. Exercise search-to-evidence and return, menu transit and Escape, keyboard selection, independent and nested expansion, text selection, Reset/zoom/pan, drafts, unavailable saving, and full-context exports at non-default zoom. Reopen a generated reviewed copy in a fresh browser page and verify original evidence and annotations together. The library’s native runner can help, but it does not inspect every authored composition or establish assistive-technology quality.

When local file opening or another native capability is administratively unavailable, state that boundary precisely. Rendering the assembled bytes inside a native browser page can establish useful layout and interaction observations; it does not establish file-origin permissions, local storage durability, operating-system handoff, or successful offline opening from an unrelated folder. Do not label those unexecuted checks as passed. Complete independent ordinary and adversarial review in the intended delivery environment before treating the report as fully qualified.


Check inner layout, not only document width. Open initially closed components, resize an embedded container inside a wide page, and verify that adjacent controls do not overlap. Long status words should stay intact, long source/code lines should scroll in their own reader, and selected records should retain their field structure and citations in expanded views. Inspect short landscape windows as well as narrow portrait layouts. Confirm that source-reader Escape returns to the original expanded figure, and that switching reading modes keeps the evidence at the reading line. Inspect the assembled file after rebuilding maintained sources; preview-only CSS changes do not qualify a reusable fix.
