# Split Testing

You SHALL deliver the decision the comparison serves: which alternative to use, a conditional choice, a supported tie, a rejection of every alternative, or an explicit statement that the evidence cannot yet decide. A testing plan is the deliverable only when the user asks for one.

Scale the comparison to the stakes and to the budget it spends: price its runs, executor families and judge calls before launching it, put bulk runs on the family with the most available budget, and add another family where its different outcomes are what the comparison needs to learn. When later runs can change the decision only under some results of earlier ones, run the cheapest decisive part first and fix in advance which results buy the rest. A choice you can settle from existing evidence or domain knowledge needs no new observations; a decision that others will rely on, or that changes standing instructions, needs observed results.

Anything that can be chosen between is an alternative, including a direction that holds alternatives of its own, and observations may come from any source: runs of the trial runtime, or people's ratings and choices, analytics, measurements, and evidence that already exists. A decision nothing can measure is compared by its reasons against stated criteria. Before observing, fix what each alternative is observed on, which way is better on each measure (or that neither is), the units, and the cases the claim will cover. Record each observation with its alternative, measure, case, and the unit it came from (a repeat, rater, session, or person); where only totals exist, record them as totals and invent no per-unit values. Nested alternatives are compared within each group and between groups, and a pooled result is read after the parts it pools. Where criteria must be traded off, the weights are the decider's own: show them and the arithmetic, and compute no total otherwise.

When you observe, the comparison SHALL hold these properties:

- **Criteria before results.** Acceptance conditions, success measures, and the direction of better come from what the consumer must accomplish and are fixed before you inspect outcomes; later amendments stay visible. Alternatives first pass acceptance; only survivors are ranked, because every alternative can be inadequate.
- **Blind, isolated contributors.** Each worker, candidate, rater, and judge receives only its own assignment and the originals it needs, never another contributor's output, the expected winner, or your hypothesis. Isolation comes from the runtime and the environment, not from asking a participant to ignore what it can see.
- **Repeated observation.** Where behavior varies, each alternative is observed on each case enough times to tell its result from luck, in an interleaved order. Report counts with intervals where the sample supports them, and as plain counts where it does not; a handful of clean runs bounds a failure rate only loosely. Runs go where outcomes vary: a case every alternative passes, or every one fails, can show no difference. Before a large run, estimate from earlier or pilot results whether its size can detect the difference worth acting on.
- **Qualified instruments.** A check, judge, or metric definition counts only after it gives the right verdict on a known-good and a known-bad case and on a sample of real outputs read by hand, since an instrument that passes reference cases can still misjudge what executors actually produce. Deterministic checks on the resulting state come before model judgment. A judge sees no labels that reveal which alternative produced a response, and a judge from a different model family counters self-preference.
- **Real conditions.** Cases reproduce the conditions that produce the behavior in question, including ordinary successes as well as failures, and the requirements a case checks appear in what the executor was given. Hidden test cases are fine; hidden requirements are not.
- **Traceable conclusions.** Every decisive claim traces to a retained native record, including whether each executor actually read or loaded the material that differs between alternatives, so an unread treatment is not mistaken for one that failed. Summaries help navigation and never replace the records they summarize.

For agent and instruction comparisons, and for non-code alternatives judged as delivered artifacts (a business plan, a recipe, a design, a pitch) or compared head-to-head, use the [trial runtime](references/trials.md): the executor writes the plan and scenarios for the question, and the runtime supplies isolation, confinement, repetition, journaling, blind judging (including blind pairwise judging), and intervals. Where the runtime cannot run the executors, because no Codex, Claude, or Gemini CLI or shell command reaches them, carry out the same [trial flow](references/trial-flow.md) with the host's own means, such as its subagents, and with the bundled scripts that still run; the properties above hold either way. Observations the runtime cannot produce are gathered under the same properties, each with its source. Use [designing comparisons](references/designing-comparisons.md) when criteria, allocation, judges, raters, nested alternatives, totals-only data, unmeasured decisions, or causal claims need more thought, and [workspaces](references/workspaces.md) when blind contributors share input copies outside the runtime.

When revising an alternative keeps moving its failure instead of removing it, question its premise and the comparison's frame before revising it again: an alternative revised against the same cases until it passes has been fitted to them, not shown sound.

When you organize a review of the conclusion, it SHALL include an independent adversarial contribution that works from the original evidence; a verdict that the work is sound is a valid result.

Your answer SHALL lead with the decision, then its grounds, its consequential limits, and what would change it. A standalone report is optional; when one serves the reader, [presenting evidence](references/presenting-evidence.md) describes the bundled visual library for trials and for any other comparison.
