# Qualification: `review-flags-excess`

What the scenario puts an agent in front of: a branch to review. `toolroom`, a std-only Python tool for a neighbourhood tool library, has a numbered `SPEC.md` (R1 to R11) and a branch, `feature/overdue-notices`, checked out, that implements R6 to R11 on top of `main` (R1 to R5). The change is two commits, 13 files, and 423 lines with its tests, and its 33 tests pass. It holds two defects against the SPEC that no test catches, three constructs that no requirement asks for and nothing in the repository needs, and two look-alikes that the SPEC does require. The request is an ordinary review request: review the branch against SPEC.md, write what you find to REVIEW.md, change no code. It says nothing about excess, size, or simplicity.

The hypothesis it makes measurable: under ordinary review instructions a reviewer reports what a change lacks and rarely what it adds beyond its requirements, so a review loop only ever asks for more, and each fix adds a mechanism. The scenario measures what a reviewer reports. It tests no fix. A pilot of 12 runs (a GPT-family and a Claude-family model, three instruction arms, two repeats) and a confirmation trial of 16 more have been read against it; "Real reviews" below gives what they showed about the reading, and the seven real reviews the reading got wrong are references here.

## The repository and its seven items

`fixture/` is `main`: the package (`models`, `data`, `cli`: the data files, the `out` command), 8 tests, `SPEC.md`, `README.md`, and a Makefile whose `check` runs the tests. `branch/` holds the files the feature branch adds or changes; `setup.sh` lays them over the fixture in two commits ("Add the fine and the overdue list (R6 to R8)", "Add the overdue command, CSV output and notices (R9 to R11)") after three commits that build `main`, and leaves the branch checked out. Git runs in `setup.sh` without the machine's own configuration, so a commit never waits on a signing agent. `SPEC.md` is the same on both and the branch does not touch it. Nothing in the repository, the history, or the prompt mentions excess, size, or simplicity.

| Item | What it is | Where on the branch | The SPEC line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Defect 1 | loans with equal days are listed by member name and loan id Z to A: `reverse=True` on the whole `(days, member, loan)` key | `toolroom/overdue.py:21` | R8: same days by member name A to Z, then loan id |
| Defect 2 | the total at the foot of a notice is the summed days times 50 cents, which ignores the cap and the supporter discount that the listed fines include | `toolroom/notices.py:15` (`CENTS_PER_DAY` at :8) | R10: the total is the sum of the listed fines; R7 gives the cap and the discount |
| Excess 1, registry | an abstract `FineRule`, a `RULES` registry, `register`, `get_rule`, and one implementation, `PerDayRule` | `toolroom/rules.py` (42 lines), used at `fines.py:24` | none; R7 defines one fine |
| Excess 2, rounding | `ROUNDING_MODES` (nearest, up, down, even), `round_div`, a `rounding` parameter through `fine_for` and `overdue_loans`, and `--rounding` | `fines.py:4-19`, `cli.py:7, 29, 43` | none; R7: rounded to the nearest cent, halves up |
| Excess 3, cache | a `ToolCache` with a 300-second TTL and hit counters around the tools table, fed by a callable that rereads the file | `toolroom/cache.py` (25 lines), used at `overdue.py:12, 19`, `cli.py:43` | none; R1: runs once, by hand or from the nightly job |

The defects are findable from the code and the SPEC alone (the sort key and R8, `total_owed` and R10) and the tests pass with both: no test has two loans with the same days, and the notice tests use a standard member under the cap. The excess constructs are working code with tests of their own, so a reviewer who stops at "it is tested" sees nothing wrong; the retry and the CSV option are the same kinds of thing (a retry loop with a sleep, an option with two modes) and a reviewer who reads R9 and R11 finds the requirement. `python3 qualify/design_check.py` shows this without the check: it lays the branch over the base in a temporary directory and finds that the branch's 33 tests pass; that on a library worked out by hand from the SPEC (seven loans, four members, today 2026-10-15) the branch lists the three loans that share 10 days as Dara, Chen, Bram, and totals Bram's notice at 27.00 and Chen's at 6.00 where R10 gives 9.00 and 3.75, and is right for Dara and everything else; that with the two lines changed the list, the CSV, and the totals are the ones worked out by hand and the 33 tests still pass; that the branch with those two lines changed agrees with a 12-line reference written from R6 to R9 (no rule class, no rounding option, no cache) on 400 random libraries, in text and CSV, so the three constructs change nothing at the defaults and the SPEC needs none of them; and that the retry makes at most three attempts one second apart, none after the last, and reports a skipped notice as R11 says.

## How the checks decide

Required (the check reads files and runs git read-only through `run.git`; it runs no agent code):

- `review_written`: `REVIEW.md` exists and says something.
- `code_unchanged`: the reviewer reviewed and did not edit. `main` and the branch hold the same files as at the start (a commit that only adds `REVIEW.md` does not count), HEAD is one of them, no tracked file differs from HEAD (`REVIEW.md` apart), and no file was added under `toolroom/` or `tests/`. Switching branches, running the tests (bytecode caches are ignored files), and scratch files elsewhere do not count; they are listed in `extra_files`.
- `defect_tie_order_found`, `defect_notice_total_found`: the review reports the defect: in one finding it names where it is (equal days, ties, member names, A to Z; the notice total) and says what is wrong (reversed, descending, Z to A; ignores the cap or the discount, is not the sum, days x 50), in the finding's own words and not in a section that records what was found right.
- `decoys_not_flagged`: the review does not report the retry (R11) or `--format` and the CSV output (R9) as unrequested, unneeded, or removable.

Measures, none of which decides a run, and the point of the scenario:

- `excess_reported` (0 to 3), and `excess_registry_reported`, `excess_rounding_reported`, `excess_cache_reported`: the review says the construct is unrequested, unneeded, or removable. They sort together in a summary, and `excess_reported` is a mean.
- `asks_to_add`, `asks_to_remove`, `asks_only_for_more`: findings that ask for something to be added (add, include, test, validate, document; "should validate"; missing, no tests, untested, does not check) and findings that ask for something to be removed (a cue of the kinds below, whether or not it names a construct; the fix of a defect such as "drop `reverse=True`" is not one), and whether the review asked for something to be added and for nothing to be removed. A finding is a heading with its paragraphs, a list item, a table row, or a line of a paragraph written one finding to a line; a summary or a section that records what was found right is not one. A review that puts several findings under one heading counts them as one, so compare the counts between arms that write alike and read `asks_only_for_more` and the `excess_*` values first.
- `decoy_retry_flagged`, `decoy_format_flagged`: which decoy a failed `decoys_not_flagged` is about.
- `review_words`, `extra_files`, `changes` (what `code_unchanged` found), and `evidence` (the clause each report rests on), which read as "mixed" in a summary and are in each run's `result.json`.

How a review is read (`hidden/review_text.py` splits it, `hidden/excess_signals.py` reads it; `check.py`'s docstring has the definitions):

- A review is cut into blocks (a heading, a list item with its continuation lines, a table row, a paragraph) and the blocks into clauses (sentences, cut again at a semicolon, at but / however / although / while / yet / unlike, and around a parenthesis of four words or more unless what it belongs to names nothing). A line that is only bold or only capitals, a short unpunctuated line over a list, and a paragraph directly above a list lead into what is under them; so does a heading. A fence that holds YAML or Markdown is read as the text it holds; a code fence is one stretch of text.
- A construct is named by its identifiers and by what reviewers call it (the registry: `FineRule`, `rules.py`, registry, plug-in, abstract base class, strategy; the rounding modes: `--rounding`, modes, half-even; the cache: `ToolCache`, cache, TTL, expiry; the retry: retry, attempts, sleep, delay, backoff; the format: `--format`, `render_csv`, CSV output, output format). A name and a cue in the same clause report the construct: a strong cue (unrequested, unnecessary, speculative, YAGNI, over-engineered, scope creep, gold-plating, dead code, "not required", "isn't in the spec", "the spec doesn't ask for", "nothing in the spec calls for", "nobody asked for", "no requirement", "only one implementation", "adds complexity for no benefit", "beyond what R7 requires", "do we need ...?"), or a removal verb that acts on the name (remove, delete, drop, inline, strip, kill, ditch, "get rid of", "can go", "X can be removed"; for the excess constructs also simplify, collapse, hard-code). Two more cues are claims that the counts of findings do not count: "outside the requested command interface", "beyond the specified behavior" (said of the interface, not of a number: "longer than the specified delay" is not one), and, for the three excess constructs only, a clause that mentions the SPEC and says "rather than" or "instead of" allowing, permitting, exposing, offering, supporting, providing, accepting, enabling, or letting ("enforce the specified rounding rule rather than allowing it to be overridden"). Said of the retry or `--format` that form asks for something else ("keep the delay fixed as R11 says, rather than letting callers set it") and reports nothing.
- A negator within five words turns a cue round ("not unnecessary", "don't remove"). A clause without a name that carries a cue and points back reports what the clauses before it named. It points back when it says "it", "they", "all three", or "this" or "that" (but not "this case", "that failure", or another word for a circumstance), describes the construct without naming it ("Remove the option and alternate policies and always apply R7", "delete the module", "drop the layer"), is a few words about nothing else, or is nothing but the cue ("remove it", "YAGNI"). A removal that describes what it removes in a clause that also names what it keeps ("remove the alternative policies and keep the specified rounding behavior") points back too, and the name it keeps is not what it removes. "The override" can only be one of the three constructs (no requirement asks for an override, so it is never the retry or `--format`), so a removal of it is about the one of the three that was named last, even when a look-alike was named since ("... in both output formats and notices. Remove the override."). A heading or lead-in with a cue ("Nobody asked for these") reports what is listed under it, unless the item approves of itself.
- Four kinds of clause are not reports on a construct they sit beside, because pilot reviews of this branch write them next to the retry. A statement that the SPEC is silent on a detail or a case ("the spec doesn't say what happens when mkdir fails", "the spec doesn't cover this case") asks for something to be decided. A statement that something required is missing ("exits without the required stderr message", "does not print the specified header", "gives up without the message R11 requires") is a defect or a gap; "adds a layer without implementing any requested behavior", "with no required behavior", and "modes that R7 does not ask for" are still claims. A removal verb in the third person ("the cache drops entries after 300 seconds", "the retry omits the sleep after the final attempt, as it should") describes code, where "drop it", "dropping the cache", and "can be dropped" recommend. And a construct that is only the reference point of "mkdir is outside the retry" or "the directory bypasses the retry" is not what the clause, or a "this" after it, is about. The base forms "match" and "follow" count as approval, like "matches" and "follows" ("R11: retries match the spec").
- A decoy report is withdrawn when its clause approves ("R11 requires it", "matches R9", "is fine"), or one of the next two clauses approves or cites the SPEC line without a cue ("looks like over-engineering, but R11 settles that"). For the excess constructs an approval in the clause does not cancel a cue, because the words of approval are about something else ("R7 asks for half-up, but `--rounding` offers four modes").
- A defect is reported when, within three consecutive clauses of one finding, a place word appears (a heading above the finding may supply it) and a fault word appears in the finding's own clauses, the clause that carries the fault does not say that all is correct, and, for the notice total, a word of what it concerns appears too (cap, discount, fines, sum, `total_owed`, days x 50). "reverse=True" alone is not a fault until the clause also says what it does.

Decisions to know when reading a run:

- The reading is by name and cue, with no judge: what the comparison needs, whether a review reports an unrequested construct, is a matter of words, and a judge would add a second, unqualified reader of the same prose. Read the `evidence` of a run before counting it, and its `REVIEW.md` when a measure surprises you.
- `decoys_not_flagged` fails a review that is right about everything else. That is the point of the decoys: a reviewer who reports whatever looks like machinery fails it, and a rise in `excess_reported` that comes with a fall in `decoys_not_flagged` is trigger-happiness, not discrimination.
- A review that never reports the defects reports little of anything, so compare the excess measures among the runs that pass `defect_*_found`.
- `excess_*` read the review, not the repository: a review that names a construct to ask for a test of it, to document it, or to say it is fine reports nothing (`names-not-excess`, `asks-for-more`).

## What the reading cannot see

- A construct named only by what it does, with none of the words above ("the tool lookups survive between calls for five minutes"), and a cue that is only implied, such as a column header ("In SPEC?") over a cell that says "No".
- A nit that shares a clause with a name reads as a report on it ("unnecessary blank line in cache.py"), and so does a remark about a detail of a decoy that happens to use a cue ("no need to make the delay configurable: R11 fixes it").
- An objection with no cue ("I'd push back on `--rounding`: four modes, but R7 only specifies one") is not a report.
- A short removal clause about something else reads as a report on the construct named just before it ("R7 fixes the rounding. Remove the unused import."), because a clause of a few words that carries a cue points back. These six sentences and the one in the next point are the seven `GAPS` in `read_reviews.py`; they print and do not fail.
- A look-alike named in passing is what the next short removal points back to: "This affects both output formats. Remove the reverse flag." is read as a report on `--format`, which fails `decoys_not_flagged`, though the sentence names the output only to say where a defect shows. Five of the 28 real reviews write "both output formats" in passing (four in a defect finding and one in the finding on the rounding option) and none is read wrongly; the one that follows it with a removal ("... in both output formats and notices. Remove the override from the command ...") is read right because an override is never a look-alike. When a run fails `decoys_not_flagged` with a short removal clause as its `evidence`, read the clause before it.
- Phrasing the words do not know. The vocabulary was grown from the reviews below, so the reviews cannot say how it does on text nobody has written. The 30 `voice-*` reviews were written in five batches of six, each read once before any word was added. The first readings were wrong for 9 of the 30: for 3 of the first six (the notice total missed twice, and all three constructs missed in a terse list that says "kill"), 2 of the next six (two decoys wrongly flagged because a table cell said "None, specified"; the rounding option missed in a YAML list), 2 of the third six (the registry missed because "fine" in "one fine rule" read as an approval, the retry wrongly flagged because the lead-in of the list above it reached the next paragraph, and a flag on the retry missed), none of the fourth six, and 2 of the last six (a bold label with a parenthesis over a list; "could be a function"). That is 15 wrong readings in 210 (7%): 12 misses and 3 wrong flags. The first 12 real reviews did no better: 2 of the 12 had a wrong measure, and one of those failed a required check it should have passed; of the next 16, 5 had a wrong measure (all misses, and none of a required check) ("Real reviews", below). Every wrong reading is fixed and pinned, and the batches that follow a fix are the ones that find the next gap. Expect about one review in four to need a word added, and more misses than wrong flags.
- The counts of findings that ask to add and to remove are rougher than the excess measures. Of the 53 findings the 12 pilot reviews are read as asking to remove something, at least 13 do not: 6 are the summary paragraph under the title ("Findings are of equal standing: ... additions no requirement asks for", or "Five findings: three correctness/spec issues and two instances of over-engineering"), which is counted as a finding; 5 are a statement that the spec is silent ("the spec does not say what should happen here"); one is "this discards both the replacement-value cap and the supporter discount", and one is "without the prescribed member-specific diagnostics". Four of the six summary paragraphs are the `both` arm's, which restate the instruction they were given, so `asks_to_remove` runs about one higher for that arm by construction. `asks_only_for_more` would not change in the pilot (every run but the two `none` GPT-family reviews has at least three findings that do ask to remove something), but compare `asks_to_remove` between arms only after reading the findings it counts. The fixes in "Real reviews" change what a clause reports, not what the counts count, so no stored count moved. That leaves one inconsistency, from the confirmation trial: "outside the requested command interface" and "enforce the specified rounding rule rather than allowing it to be overridden" report the option and are not cues that `asks_to_remove` counts, so the two reviews whose only request to remove is the second (`confirm-none-gpt-r3`, `confirm-none-gpt-r4`) read `asks_to_remove` 0 and `asks_only_for_more` true, though each asks for the option to go. Counting the two claims as cues (adding `beyond` and `alternatives` to `Clause.cue`) moves exactly those two runs' two values and nothing else in the 98 reviews read here (the 28 stored and the 70 references); it was left out so that the recheck of the stored runs changes only the values the reading of the constructs fixed.
- The fixture has a finding nobody planned. `write_notices` creates the notices directory before the per-member write, outside the retry and the skip rule of R11, and the SPEC does not say what a directory that cannot be created should do. Ten of the 12 pilot reviews report it: four GPT-family reviews list it as a finding against R10 and R11 ("bypasses retry and skip behavior"; three rate it P2), and all six Claude-family reviews call it a minor gap to decide. It is why the retry is named in so many reviews, and why the reading has to tell a gap in the retry's reach from a claim that the retry is unrequested. Neither required check depends on it.
- Before a comparison, read the `REVIEW.md` of ten runs per arm against their `evidence` and `excess_*` values. When a measure is read wrongly, add the sentence to `SNIPPETS` in `read_reviews.py`, extend the words in `hidden/excess_signals.py` until `python3 qualify/read_reviews.py` is green again, and `trial.py recheck RUN_DIR` the stored runs; no agent runs again.

## Reading a comparison

Fixed before any agent trial. Compare an arm that has the instruction under test with an arm that lacks it and is alike in everything else (model, effort, harness, plan settings), with at least 10 valid runs each. One scenario is one case: it bounds a difference loosely and says nothing of other changes or other kinds of excess. Four numbers:

- Pass rate: runs that pass all five required checks. A drop is a review that missed a defect, flagged a decoy, edited the code, or was not written.
- Among the runs that pass `defect_tie_order_found` and `defect_notice_total_found`: the mean `excess_reported`, the share with `excess_reported` 0, and the three `excess_*_reported` rates apart (the registry is a whole module and the rounding modes are a few lines, so they differ in how easy they are to see).
- Among the same runs: `asks_to_add` against `asks_to_remove`, and the share with `asks_only_for_more`. The hypothesis predicts few reports of excess and many reviews that only ask for more.
- `decoys_not_flagged` among the same runs. An instruction reduces what a review misses about excess when `excess_reported` rises without `decoys_not_flagged` or the defect rates falling.

## Results

`evals/plans/qualify-review-flags-excess.json` (command arms, no model, no judge; 80 arms, the 79 references above and `noop`, run with `--repeats 2`): every run ended on its own, and every arm matched its row in the tables above, with the verdict, the failed required checks, `excess_reported`, the three `excess_*_reported` values, the decoys flagged, and `extra_files` as written, and nothing else failed. The second repeat read exactly as the first for all 80 arms. `python3 qualify/verdicts.py RUN_DIR` makes that comparison and prints "80 arms, 2 repeat(s) each, 0 with a verdict or a value other than the one written". The 160 runs took 17 seconds at `--jobs 8` (`~/.cache/agent-trials/qualify-review-flags-excess-20261015-174653`); the check takes about 0.1 second a run, and `setup.sh` a tenth of a second alone and about 2 seconds with eight runs starting at once. Fourteen arms failed and 66 passed, and only the arms marked pass above passed.

Needs bubblewrap, python3, and git. Two scripts need no trial: `python3 qualify/design_check.py` (the facts of the repository above) and `python3 qualify/read_reviews.py` (223 sentences with what they report, a grid of 7,924 more, the 70 reviews, and the three re-typeset copies of each; it exits 1 when one is read differently from what is written there). Run both after changing `hidden/` or the fixture.

The grid sets four ways of naming each construct against 50 ways of saying it is unrequested or removable and 25 of saying something else about it (that it is required, tested, fine, or needs a test), each as a sentence, a bullet, a numbered note, and a table row: 2,400 of 2,400 reported for the excess constructs, 1,600 of 1,600 flagged when said of a decoy, 800 of 800 decoy sentences without a claim left alone, and 528 of 528 such sentences about the excess constructs (the 11 of the 25 that cite no SPEC line); and, for each defect, 260 of 260 ways of saying what is wrong found and 200 of 200 sentences that call it right or ask for a test not found. Four more rows came from the pilot: removals that describe their object after a sentence that names it (88 of 88 reported or flagged), a statement that the spec is silent on a case after a sentence that names a construct (180 of 180 reported nothing), the same after "mkdir is outside the retry" (60 of 60), and the 51 audited sentences that call nothing unrequested (204 of 204 reported nothing). Eight more rows came from the confirmation trial, 1,112 cases: removing "the override" after a sentence that names an excess construct (60 of 60 reported), after one that names a decoy (40 of 40 flagged nothing), and with a decoy named in passing between (180 of 180 reported the construct and flagged no decoy); "outside" or "beyond" the requested interface, said of an excess construct (240 of 240 reported) and of a decoy (160 of 160 flagged); "rather than allowing" and its kin, held up against the SPEC, of an excess construct (384 of 384 reported); and a removal that describes its object and keeps a named thing, for the registry and the rounding option (24 of 24 reported) and with a decoy as the thing kept (24 of 24 flagged nothing). Another 32 sentences joined the row of those that call nothing unrequested (236 of 236 reported nothing). The grid was grown with the words, like the reviews, so it shows what the reading accepts and not how it generalises.

Not made deterministic: whether a review that names a construct only by what it does, or objects without a cue, "reports" it; how findings are counted when a review puts several under one heading; and any phrasing the words have not met.
