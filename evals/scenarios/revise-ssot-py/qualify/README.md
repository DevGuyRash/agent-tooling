# Qualification: `revise-ssot-py`

A change to a business rule that earlier changes already copied, with the copies drifted. The repository is `circdesk`, a public library's circulation tools in Python (standard library only), with tests, a README, and six commits of history. Three commands charge overdue fines: `receipt` (the desk receipt, `circdesk/receipt.py`, where the rule was first written), `notices` (the overnight notices, `notices.py`), and `account` (the kiosk's account view, `account.py`). The rule is cents a day and the most an item can owe, by category, and a grace period. The notices and the kiosk each got a copy of it, and two later policy changes each reached only some copies, so today each copy is wrong in a different part: the receipt caps children's items at 2.50, the notices give 3 days' grace, and the kiosk caps media at 10.00, where `docs/fines.md` says 3.00, 2 days, and 12.50. The history shows each slip (`git log -p circdesk docs`).

The request is the board's next change, which Mira already wrote into `docs/fines.md` (adult items 0.35 a day instead of 0.30, and a new `device` category at 2.40 a day up to 48.00), to be charged at the desk, in the notices, and on the kiosk. The prompt asks for that and nothing more: it names neither the copies, nor that they disagree, nor how to fix them. The policy page says the desk, the notices, and the kiosk all charge by it and that from 1 November every fine is worked out by it, so charging the policy includes the parts each copy has wrong. Editing all three copies, and fixing each one's drift, passes the visible tests and every hidden case today, and leaves the next change to be made three times.

## How the checks decide

The check runs `python3 -m circdesk` from fresh copies of the agent's tree (`run.copy_workdir`, run only through `run.sandboxed`), on exports written from `hidden/cases.json` (made by `hidden/make_cases.py`), and compares with `hidden/reference.py`. The reference prints what the fixture prints, under any rule passed in. Run under each fixture copy's own old rule, it matches the fixture's actual output on all 58 hidden cases. Expected output comes from the reference at check time.

- `existing_tests_pass`: the fixture's tests that the request does not touch (`tests/test_loans.py`, `test_money.py`, `test_cli.py`, and `tests/data/`), placed beside the agent's code in a directory of their own (`circdesk_regression_tests/`), pass with `python3 -m unittest discover`. The fixture's `test_receipt.py`, `test_notices.py`, and `test_account.py` pin the old adult rate, so updating them is part of the request, and they are not among these.
- `existing_behavior_kept`: 24 hidden cases of behavior the request does not touch give what the fixture gives. They cover exports with a byte-order mark (as the library system writes them), columns in another order with values padded and categories in any case, refusals with exit status 1 and `circdesk:` (unknown loan, a loan still out, a missing file or column, a bad date, unknown patron, and a category the policy does not know in every command, the notices' and the kiosk's on a loan still out and inside its grace period, so checking the grace period before the category is caught too), `No notices.` and `No fines.`, the kiosk's block at exactly 10.00, and bad command lines with exit status 2.
- `new_policy_charged`: 21 hidden cases where the fixture's three copies, each with the request applied, would agree (no item exactly 3 days late, no children's item owing more than 2.50, no media item owing more than 10.00). Each command prints exactly what `docs/fines.md` gives: adult items at 0.35, device items at 2.40 up to 48.00, and the unchanged parts.
- `drift_resolved`: 13 hidden cases where those copies disagree. These are items exactly 3 days late, children's items owing up to 3.00, and media items owing up to 12.50, run through every command. Each command prints what `docs/fines.md` gives, so the drift was resolved to the documented rule and not to whichever copy came first.
- `all_follow_rule_edits`: after the agent's change, the rule is edited again twelve ways, one at a time, the way the board would edit it next year. These are the adult rate up and down (0.35 to 0.40 and to 0.30), the device rate up (2.40 to 2.75), the device most down (48.00 to 36.00), the children's most up (3.00 to 3.50), the media most down and up (12.50 to 11.00 and to 15.00), and the grace period up and down (2 to 3 and to 1 days). Then every daily rate goes up by 5 cents at once, "2 days late or less owes nothing" becomes "less than 2 days late owes nothing", and "from the third day late every day counts, the first two included" becomes "only the days past the grace period count", an edit of how the parts of the rule combine. After each edit, every policy and drift case is run again against the reference under the edited rule. An edit passes when one place in the agent's tree, edited, makes the receipt, the notices, and the kiosk all give the edited rule's answers. A copy of any value, of the grace comparison, or of the multiplication of the days by the daily rate fails: the edit that moves one command leaves the copy behind. The last edit is what catches a partial consolidation that shares the table, the grace period, and even the grace test, and leaves each command to work the fine out itself (`cap-copy`). Before every run, `check.py` verifies that each policy case is one the copies agree on and each drift case one they disagree on. It also verifies that every edit changes some hidden output of every command, and that every daily rate shows in some output of every command under the price rise. If any of that fails, it refuses to run, and the run is invalid.
- `one_rule_definition`: at most one of the program's files holds the fines table, meaning at least 3 of the amounts 0.15, 1.25, 3.00, and 7.00 outside comments and docstrings. 3.00 and 7.00 written as whole euros (`3`, `7`) count inside a bracketed structure, at most two levels out, that holds another of the rule's amounts (a JSON object of rates, a dict or tuple of rows), so `json-numbers`' table counts and so would a stale copy in that spelling. Neither the request nor the drift touches those amounts, so an old copy left behind unused still carries them.

The program's files are the Python outside `tests/`, `test/`, `docs/`, `test_*.py`, `*_test.py`, `setup.py`, and `conftest.py`, plus every file that code names by a string literal ending in the file's name (`Path(__file__).with_name("fines.json")`, `.../"docs"/"fines.md"`), wherever it lives, followed through. Package metadata is never among them. A document such as `docs/fines.md` counts as a definition only when an edit lands in it, so code that merely mentions the page (`good`'s `POLICY_PAGE = "docs/fines.md"`) does not make it a second table. Other data files outside tests and docs are tried last as places, and count as definitions only when an edit lands in them.

How an edit's place is found. The canonical home is wherever one edit moves every command. The fixture's names only order the tries.

- For a value edit, every occurrence of the value in the program's files is a candidate, in its usual spellings. An amount in cents can be `35` or `0.35`; `4800`, `4_800`, `48_00`, `48.00`, `48.0`, or `48`; `240`, `2_40`, `2.40`, or `2.4`. A whole amount written whole becomes the new amount's short spelling when the new one is not whole (the children's most `3` becomes `3.5`). The grace period is `2`. Candidates come in this order: inside a declaration with one of the fixture's names for that part (`DAILY_FINE`, `FINES`, `PER_DAY`; `MAX_FINE`, `FINES`, `MOST`; `GRACE_DAYS`, `GRACE`, `GRACE_PERIOD`), then in files where an earlier edit landed, then other Python, then data the program names, then other data.
- The price rise is made where the adult-rate edit landed. It edits every daily rate in the smallest bracketed structure around that place that holds all four (the `RATES` dict, a JSON object, a tuple of rows), or the whole file when none does (a Markdown table). It uses the spelling family found there, and edits each spelling once.
- For the grace comparison edit, every `<`, `<=`, `>`, and `>=` in the program's Python outside comments and strings is a candidate, turned between strict and inclusive. Then each line holding several comparisons is tried with all of them turned at once. Candidates on a line that names what the grace edits landed in come first (`GRACE_DAYS`, `FREE_DAYS`, a key such as `"grace_days"`). Then come candidates in files where the value edits landed, then code that imports or names those files, then other code.
- For the combination edit, each operand `X` of every binary `*` in the program's Python outside comments and strings is a candidate, turned into `(X - 2)`, 2 being the grace period (`days * rate.per_day` becomes `(days - 2) * rate.per_day`); number literals are left out. Candidates on a line naming what the rate edits landed in come first (`per_day`, `RATES`), then the files as for the comparison edit, and an operand naming days before the other.
- At most 12 candidates are tried per value edit and 20 per comparison or combination edit, each in a fresh copy. A rule moved to a new module, renamed, re-shaped, turned into a JSON file, or read from `docs/fines.md` itself is followed by behavior. A copy cannot pass, because no single edit moves all three commands.

An edit is followed only when every case is right under it, so a tree that is wrong before any edit (`wrong`, `wrong-drift`, `copies-drift-kept`) follows none. Read `new_policy_charged` and `drift_resolved` first. Nothing is timed except command limits. Each command of the unedited run may take 60 seconds. Each command of an edited run may take ten times the slowest command of the unedited run, at least 20 seconds and at most 60. Six commands run at a time. A command over its limit is reported as `name(timeout)` in the `*_cases_failed` measures and counted in `command_timeouts`. When the unedited run has a timeout, the edits are not tried.

Its limits:

- Some rules cannot be found by any candidate, and such a run fails `all_follow_rule_edits` with `rule_edits_followed_by_receipt` below 12/12; read that run before counting it. Examples are a grace period written as `days < GRACE + 1`, whose strict form is `days < GRACE`; a grace period held as the first day charged (`FIRST_CHARGED_DAY = 3` with `days < FIRST_CHARGED_DAY`, the `from-day` arm, correct on every hidden case), which the grace-period edits, looking for `2`, and the comparison edit cannot reach, so it follows 9/12; and rates computed from something else.
- Grace-period candidates are every standalone `2`, so code dense with other `2`s ahead of the rule's place can use up the 12 tries.
- The cap applied on its own has no edit: a design that shares the multiplication of the days by the rate but has each command apply `min(..., most)` itself passes. The multiplication copied, with or without the cap, fails the combination edit.
- The categories themselves and the kiosk's block threshold have no edit. A command that keeps its own category list while sharing the amounts passes; that would show as a refusal for a category added later.
- A data file that no shipped code names and no edit lands in is not counted.

Apart from `from-day`, which shows the second example, none of the reference behaviors below hits any of these limits.

Measures, deciding nothing:

- `rule_edits_followed_by_receipt`, `_notices`, `_account`, and `_all` (out of 12).
- `rule_edit_sites`: where each edit landed, or the places that moved each command separately.
- `rule_edit_tries`.
- `table_copies` and `table_files`.
- `rule_module`: where the adult-rate edit landed, or `-` when no place moved the receipt.
- `table_amounts_elsewhere`: amounts of the table, and the daily rates, found in the program's files other than the rule's module.
- `consumers_reach_rule_module`: whether `receipt.py`, `notices.py`, and `account.py` reach the rule's module through imports and named files.
- `policy_cases_failed`, `drift_cases_failed`, `existing_cases_failed`, `command_timeouts`, `slowest_command_seconds`.
- `suite_passes`: the agent's own `tests/` as it left them.
- `test_files_changed`, `docs_fines_changed`, `files_changed`, and `commits_added`.

Checks take 23 to 76 seconds per run on a host shared with other trials (load average about 40 on 24 CPUs); the copies use up their tries. Run directory: `~/.cache/agent-trials/ssotfix-qualify-revise-ssot-py` (17 arms as above).

## Notes

The fixture's tests never cover the drifted inputs, which is how the drift went unnoticed. The hidden exports were made so that every edit changes at least one output of every command. Examples are a device item at 19 days (under its cap at 2.40, over it at 36.00), items exactly 2 and 3 days late, a children's item at 24 days, and media items at 9 and 13 days; every item more than 2 days late and under its cap shows the combination edit. `check.py` verifies that before every run. The check raises, making the run invalid rather than failed, when the confined `python3` is missing or older than 3.10. The qualification scripts lay files over the tree with `cp -R`.
