# ADR 0017: Separate option evidence, artifact review, and decision authority

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-16

## Decision

Keep three semantic roles distinct in comparative work:

- evidence about the alternatives or systems under comparison;
- review that improves or judges an artifact describing that evidence; and
- the preference, utility, risk posture, taste, or authority that selects among a real frontier.

A reviewer panel may find an omission and thereby improve a report. That is evidence about the review-assisted production system, not new evidence about the alternatives. Reviewer agreement does not supply values or authorization belonging to an absent user, maker, operator, or decision owner. Apply only maker-supplied or delegated values, observe the rightful decision role, or preserve the result as conditional.

Preserve distributions and interactions that can change the choice. Equal totals or averages do not establish equivalent behavior when profiles differ and no governing frequency, weight, loss, or invariance makes the difference immaterial.

## Why

A held-out three-arm Split Testing comparison used a deterministic cold-storage procurement with a real residual stewardship frontier. Three fresh executions each received the old candidate, a replication-only correction, or the full revision. Nine fresh counterbalanced blind reviewers judged the matched board reports using an independently validated candidate-blind rubric.

The result was heterogeneous rather than a stable winner. All three reviewers preferred the old arm in replicate one because the full revision flattened generator starts with equal totals but unequal case profiles: `0/2/2/0` versus `1/1/1/1`. In replicate two, one reviewer preferred the replication-only arm and two retained a three-way tie. All three preferred the full revision in replicate three because its report made that profile difference visible.

The full revision obtained the third-replicate detail through an inner rubric validator and three anonymous semantic reviewers. Those reviewers all returned the same non-dominated frontier; they did not provide the food bank's values. The outer reviewers explicitly gave the inner ceremony no credit and preferred the report only for the factual profile it made salient. The assisted run used 1,036,585 input tokens and 546 seconds, versus 347,834 and 181 seconds for the old arm and 372,405 and 199 seconds for replication-only. Across all three replicates the full revision used 1,806,183 input tokens, about 65 percent more than the old arm's 1,094,721, without a stable quality advantage.

The useful cause is therefore not “panels are bad” or “always show generator profiles.” The inner review rescued an available but omitted distinction. It did not add option evidence or authorize the board's tradeoff. The foundational repair is to preserve decision-relevant distributions, locate preference authority, and attribute any rescue and cost to the review-assisted condition. A later prospective comparison must establish whether that earlier correction retains the report benefit without the panel detour.

A subsequent refuge recheck reproduced the failure more directly. One agent had already verified the complete deterministic frontier, then created a blind profile and rubric, launched reviewers, repaired a unit typo in that profile, relaunched review, and added three more report audits. The five calls consumed 1,339,447 input tokens versus 288,501 for its matched no-panel run, while blind outer review found no new option evidence. Repairing an intermediate created solely by the review layer is self-generated process cost, not task improvement.

Driftlight later showed both sides of this boundary in one prospective trial. Assisted reports were materially better because they retained and exposed source-grounded tradeoffs that controls omitted; all nine blind comparisons preferred them, and independent recomputation preserved those reasons. One assisted execution nevertheless added an internal rubric, validator, and three reviewers after the simulator evidence already fixed the factual frontier. That panel helped the delivered artifact, but it did not observe players, create new option evidence, or acquire the producer's unresolved values. The run was the most expensive treatment by a wide margin. The trial therefore supports crediting review-assisted artifact quality while charging its cost and refusing to relabel it as evidence about the alternatives or authority to choose among them.

## Consequences

Experiments state what each review layer can observe and whether it judges alternatives, artifacts, or downstream use. Reports do not relabel artifact QA as option evidence or reviewer preference as maker authority. A rescued output may establish value for the combined system while leaving the earlier causal defect unresolved.

Reopen this decision when a reviewer population is itself the authorized decision role, when a panel observes a distinct real population or outcome, or when prospective evidence shows that an apparently duplicated layer improves criterion validity more economically than a simpler earlier control.
