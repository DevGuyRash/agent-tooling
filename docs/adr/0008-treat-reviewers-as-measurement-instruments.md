# ADR 0008: Treat reviewers as measurement instruments

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-15

## Decision

Semantic reviewers are part of the evaluation system, not an independent source of truth. Preserve the concealed candidate mapping, presentation order, exact judgment, rubric, reviewer identity, model family, and other decision-changing view state long enough to test whether a preference tracks the candidate or the instrument.

Counterbalance or randomize presentation when position can matter, but do not assume this removes bias. After reveal, a preference that follows an anonymous label, position, formatting artifact, or reviewer-family signature cannot support a candidate ranking. Preserve it as evidence about the measurement system and use another valid basis, a redesigned review, or an unresolved conclusion. Nominal reviewer count is not independent evidence when reviewer errors or preferences are correlated.

Counterbalancing must identify the effect at the level where it will be interpreted. For matched multi-way outputs, a schedule that swaps two slots while leaving one condition in a fixed slot cannot separate that condition from position. Aggregate balance across different cases does not repair the within-case confound. Use enough independently assigned views to distinguish candidate-following from position-following judgments; no particular permutation count is universal.

Even a position-identifiable, condition-blind judgment is not truth authority. Before using its preference as condition evidence, verify that its decisive candidate attribution exists and that the premise is correct under the worker-visible contract and trusted outcome evidence. A reviewer that rewards a fabricated finding or penalizes warranted uncertainty measured the wrong property. Preserve the judgment as evaluator evidence, but exclude the preference from the quality conclusion. A private oracle earns precedence only after it is validated against the public task and governing authority.

## Why

In a fresh six-review comparison on 2026-08-15, every reviewer selected anonymous Candidate B across three matched pairs. Candidate order was independently reversed between the medium- and high-effort reviewers for each pair. Reveal showed three preferences for the Split Testing candidate and three for control: every preference followed the second position, not the underlying condition. Counting the six judgments as a decisive vote would have manufactured a winner; retaining the mappings turned the same run into direct evidence of an evaluator-position effect.

This repository observation agrees with broader empirical results that model judges can be position-sensitive and that panels can supply far fewer independent votes than their headcount suggests. [SCOPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13110v3) uses order-averaged preference as an uncertainty signal for calibrated pairwise judging. [Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800v1) found strongly correlated errors in a nine-model panel. These studies support the boundary; the repository's own concealed-mapping result is the evidence for applying it here.

A later three-way Skill Auditor review repeated the failure in a subtler form. Five of six fresh reviewers selected anonymous slot B and the sixth tied A/B. The design had reversed A and C but kept the same condition in B for both reviewers of each matched output set. B represented a different condition in each replicate, producing an apparently balanced aggregate ranking while leaving every within-replicate comparison confounded. The ranking was discarded and the frozen artifacts were reassigned to a schedule in which every condition changes position within its matched set.

The corrected nine-review allocation removed that position confound but exposed a different judge failure. Five reviewers preferred unguided audits, two preferred the revised auditor, and two tied. The precommitted target truth and executable evidence showed that all three unguided audits promoted unestablished host or installer semantics into demonstrated High findings, while two of three revised audits correctly kept those consequences conditional and all revised audits found both hard source defects. Several reviewers noticed the overclaim yet still rewarded the broader report. The raw ranking therefore remained reviewer evidence rather than a quality verdict. Blinding fixed identity exposure; it did not fix truth alignment.

A later incident-handoff review again separated preference from correctness. Across nine blinded judgments, unguided audits were the sole preference five times, the revised auditor once, and the two conditions tied three times. Independent condition-blind fact checks then verified a maker-bound ordering contradiction that some reviewers had dismissed, while also confirming that two revised audits cited probe files missing from their delivered artifacts and that controls were generally more recoverable. The evidence supported specific causal repairs on both sides; it did not support turning either the raw vote or the private oracle into an unquestioned winner.

The CloseKit comparison supplied a stronger falsification of raw preference. Six fresh blind reviewers preferred the older audit four times and the revised audit twice. Separate condition-blind fact checkers found both revised preferences supported, but found every older-audit preference unsound as written because its decisive rationale discounted verified authority-boundary or failure-state omissions. The fact checks also preserved a real defect in the revised audits: some additional observations were assigned severity beyond the target's adopted guarantees. Reviewer validation therefore changed the conclusion from a misleading four-to-two ranking into two independent corrections—retain the stronger discovery and repair its disposition logic.

The Harborlight review exposed reviewer attribution and match structure at the same time. Two reviewers credited one anonymous proposal with concrete behaviors present only in another proposal, and an earlier review pass assumed independently randomized sample numbers represented the same case. The resulting prose was coherent and the judgments were committed before reveal, but neither preference could support a condition claim. A reviewer needs the smallest condition-neutral match identity required by the decision, and the controller must still resolve decisive factual attributions after reveal.

The same rule applies to instrument validators. A fresh high-effort validator correctly found one remaining contradiction in a corrected paper-game target, but also rejected the target because it claimed North failed to define how shared prizes count in the final tie-break. The exact sentence it requested was already present in the supplied file and matched the controller copy byte-for-byte. The valid correction was retained; the fabricated attribution was excluded. Independent review reduced correlated author error, but only source reconciliation made the validation trustworthy.

A later pre-exposure validator saw three deliberately harmful treatment instructions and classified their predicted output failures as defects in the experiment itself. The public task and rubric were coherent, and those treatment-versus-target conflicts were exactly what candidate execution was meant to expose. The controller retained the false rejection, narrowed the validator's decision boundary, and obtained a fresh validation before exposing candidates. A validator must distinguish an invalid measurement system from a valid system capable of measuring a bad treatment; independence alone does not supply that distinction.

A later Dispatch comparison produced an apparent seven-to-two blind preference for an older auditor. Three fresh condition-blind fact checkers then reconciled each matched set's decisive rationales against the anonymous artifacts and current primary host sources. One matched set's calibration and evidence preference survived. In another, reviewers penalized the canonical `plugin:skill` namespace; in the third, all three preferences relied materially on a supposed scalar `defaultPrompt` incompatibility that the cited raw parser explicitly accepts and on a Claude path hazard promoted beyond the missing installed execution trace. The immediate hold-release conclusion remained true, but the aggregate preference no longer supported a condition ranking. The seven votes were retained as evidence about judge susceptibility to fluent, source-adjacent false premises rather than used to optimize the auditor toward those premises.

The next matched Dispatch round made the correction operational. Nine blind judgments produced a five-to-four raw preference for the older auditor, including a unanimous three-review preference for the revision in one replicate. Three fresh condition-blind fact-checkers independently reconciled each replicate. The revision's unanimous win collapsed because every reviewer treated one plausible resolution of an ambiguous starter prompt as uniquely correct and dismissed an MIT notice finding whose governing license terms were actually incorporated by the target declaration. Two qualified older-audit preferences survived in another replicate and all three survived in the last because their decisive evidence and repair-bound claims matched the retained artifacts. The custody and fact-check layers therefore converted a nearly even vote into a materially different conclusion: the proposed revision had not earned release.

The correction later failed inside a truth-checker itself. Three fresh checkers assessed revised Dispatch audits after the auditor had stopped rejecting scalar `defaultPrompt`. Two independently confirmed that the current raw contract accepts a string. The third read a normalized `Option<Vec<String>>` field and array-shaped example as proof that raw input must be a list, then reported the auditor's correct non-finding as an omitted defect. The repeated error remained fluent, source-adjacent, and false. A fact-check layer is another semantic instrument; its premise must still be followed through the real consumer before it can overturn the artifact under review.

The eDNA comparison then showed correlated error originating in the rubric rather than candidate position or reviewer attribution. Three counterbalanced blind reviewers all preferred the assisted audit. Fresh condition-blind fact-checkers reproduced the target and audit evidence, but overturned two preferences because the rubric had promoted a repository design lens into target authority and allowed it to outweigh harder omitted boundaries. The third preference survived only with the same authority correction and a required no-clobber addition. Blinding, counterbalancing, Notes, free-form rationale, a fresh author, and an independent validator all operated as designed; they faithfully propagated the same invalid criterion. Reviewer controls reduce some biases, not a shared measurement premise.

## Consequences

Reports interpret judgments only after resolving them to underlying candidates and preserve order effects, abstentions, ties, and disagreement. A majority, score total, or larger same-family panel does not repair a compromised measurement. Deterministic checks, domain experts, user judgment, fresh reviewer designs, or calibrated methods may provide a stronger basis depending on the actual decision; no one remedy is mandatory across all domains.

Reopen this decision when the review modality, host, model family, presentation interface, or calibration evidence changes enough that position and correlated-error controls no longer affect the supported claim.
