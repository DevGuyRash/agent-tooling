# ADR 0010: Bind evaluation claims to actual exposure and named concealment

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-16

## Decision

An evaluation claim is limited by what each independently assigned unit actually encountered. An execution that receives one candidate or one case can support observations about that exposure; it cannot be counted as choosing among unseen candidates or as covering cases it never received. Grouping outputs for later review does not create a shared denominator retroactively. Preserve the assignment and exposure structure in summaries, comparisons, and durable evidence.

The information supplied before an observation must also match the property being claimed. Disclosing a required answer or evaluation criterion may be necessary for conformance or design work, but the resulting response cannot establish unaided discovery, recall, comprehension, surprise, or preference. Separate the observation or narrow the claim rather than relabeling prompted analysis as an unprompted outcome.

Describe blinding by the identity or expectation actually concealed and the inference that boundary protects. Randomized condition labels do not make authorship, candidate identity, treatment content, or an answer blind when those remain visible or semantically obvious. Partial concealment may still be valuable; it does not justify a broader validity claim.

## Why

In a fresh non-software Split Testing comparison, one candidate report said that all six participant simulations selected the proposed direction. Its retained allocation and prompts showed that each participant had assessed only one assigned concept and had made no cross-concept choice. The experimental machinery was inspectable, but the report widened six local assessments into a nonexistent comparative denominator.

The same trial described agent judgments as evidence about one-view recall even though the worker task disclosed the required response before concept exposure. Those outputs could support formative conformance analysis, not unaided recall. More repetitions and reviewers amplified the mislabeled measurement without repairing it.

Several retained reviewer views also hid condition mappings while leaving concept names or distinctive headings visible. Fact-checkers disagreed about whether that invalidated the entire comparison because the reports used “blind” without naming what was concealed. The correct conclusion was narrower: assignment concealment remained evidence against condition-label bias, while concept-identity unmasking limited claims about concept-blind review.

A later paper-game preflight found that one candidate retained its own prior review machinery and judgments. Its first disposition proposed removing source identities and prior process evidence to restore a generically “blind” view. That would have erased the instruction-induced detour the comparison existed to measure. The corrected interface instead named condition-assignment as the concealed identity, retained consequential process evidence, declared that content could permit condition inference, and made the common frozen rubric the sole reviewer authority. Blinding is not a license to sanitize a treatment's observable outcome.

Across six blind report comparisons, the Split Testing-assisted arm was preferred once, control three times, with one tie and one no-reliable-result. The assisted arm used about 2.17 times the mean input context. The decisive lesson was not that custody or repetition is useless; it was that stronger custody cannot make a widened denominator or contaminated observation measure the intended outcome.

## Consequences

Experiment reports retain the unit, candidate, case, prompt exposure, and grouping needed to audit every material denominator. A report states whether an observation measures generation, conformance, recognition, recall, preference, or another property at the strength its prompt permits.

Review records name the concealed mapping and preserve semantic unmasking risks. “Blind” is not used as an undifferentiated quality label. When exposure or concealment does not support the claimed property, preserve the useful narrower evidence and rerun only the missing observation rather than discarding unrelated facts or averaging the defect away.

Remove identity-bearing content only when it is irrelevant presentation metadata and the meaning-preserving projection remains auditable. When process, authorship, or self-identification is part of the observed outcome, retain it and narrow the concealment claim instead of editing the candidate into a cleaner result.

Reopen this decision when a measurement interface can independently establish a broader exposure or concealment boundary than the retained prompt, allocation, and artifact evidence currently show.
