# ADR 0007: Use comparative evidence to correct foundations

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-15

## Decision

A comparative evaluation is not complete merely because it ranks alternatives. For every material gain, regression, or absence of effect, trace the evidence through the condition, the behavior it changed, and the resulting outcome far enough to identify the earliest actionable cause supported by the observations. Use that evidence to preserve, reduce, redesign, or remove the intervention.

When rival explanations could change the correction, distinguish them with retained traces, counterexamples, condition reduction, or a fresh targeted comparison. Do not convert a plausible root-cause story, reviewer majority, or repeated surface symptom into fact. A valid no-difference or harm result is useful evidence and must remain visible.

Treat the diagnosis and repair as separate claims. Where feasible, alter the earliest alleged cause without changing unrelated decision-relevant conditions and establish that the predicted execution behavior and outcome change without a material regression. Recheck the exposing boundary and independent representative work before generalizing the repair. Without intervention or held-out confirmation, keep the causal or generalization claim provisional even when blind reviewers prefer the revised artifact.

## Why

The Skill Auditor redevelopment initially increased context and evaluation ceremony without improving audit quality; blind comparisons exposed missed executable defects and no reliable task gain. A later, reduced outcome-driven design was consistently preferred in a fresh comparison only after the observed failures were traced to instruction-induced detours, weak executable grounding, and measurement artifacts. The improvement came from changing the instruction system, not polishing the report shape.

That distinction is consistent with recent external evidence, while remaining grounded here in the repository trials. [CausalFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25338v1) reports that validated minimal counterfactual repairs outperform heuristic refinement in several agent-task families. [OpenAI's coding-evaluation audit](https://openai.com/index/separating-signal-from-noise-coding-evaluations/) found that strict tests, underspecified or misleading prompts, and low-coverage checks can manufacture benchmark outcomes. Neither source makes a local diagnosis true; both support demanding intervention and measurement validity before calling a surface improvement foundational.

The Split Testing trials produced the same lesson from another direction. A candidate improved one open-ended decision yet regressed on a deterministic code boundary. That heterogeneous result invalidated a broad “better” claim and led to stronger eligibility, counterexample, and prompt-purity controls. Later trials then measured the revised system rather than hiding the failed run.

A later portable-skill audit comparison eliminated the old Skill Auditor from all six blind preferences but split evenly between the redevelopment and unguided audits. The weaker candidate run found many real executable defects yet promoted one unresolved `highest_severity` interpretation into an implementation failure. The correction therefore belongs at the authority boundary—an unsettled target meaning remains ambiguity—not in a longer defect inventory or stricter report form.

The next dual-host plugin comparison exposed the same cause across a different surface. Candidate audits repeatedly applied the portable Agent Skills directory/name rule to a custom “Codex-style” package that had not adopted the official host contract, while some missed the package-local catalog-to-router contradiction that changed a critical payload's result. The foundational repair is to establish the governing consumer before importing external rules and then follow the target's own declared handoff into executable behavior.

A later three-arm Quill comparison showed that removing procedural audit ceremony was not sufficient. The reduced candidate was preferred in only one of six blind judgments; the prior auditor was preferred three times and an unguided control twice. Two candidate runs found every planted defect, but a third made a shipped broken console entry point conditional, and the condition less reliably explored independent failure boundaries once one blocker had already decided the disposition. The correction is not to restore a finite audit checklist. It is to make two semantic outcomes explicit: an adopted declaration is a target claim until release scope excludes it, and the first blocker ends unnecessary task-value proof without ending inspection of other repair-changing surfaces.

The subsequent Braid round could not be used to rank auditors because its public brief implied both capabilities were required on every host while the target contract and private oracle allowed either capability or both. Auditors predictably found the manufactured gap. That failure corrected the evaluation foundation—public task, target authority, oracle, checker, and rubric must mean the same thing—rather than prompting another auditor instruction aimed at the resulting surface symptom.

After that instrument was corrected, a valid three-arm Braid comparison still preferred unguided audits in four of six blind judgments, preferred the redevelopment once, and tied them once. The redeveloped audits found the two unconditional executable defects, but repeatedly promoted an undocumented file list or a direct-launch probe into a hard host failure and inconsistently traced a published capability through the package's actual adapter and output. The shared cause was earlier than finding coverage: the auditor had not cleanly separated artifact presence, contract adoption, consumer semantics, and observable fulfillment. The next correction therefore changes that authority model and handoff trace rather than adding package-field examples, severity rules, or report sections.

The next incident-handoff comparison showed why even correct authority language was not the end of the causal chain. Revised audits found the hard verifier failures and better distinguished an unjustified reasoning ritual from a legitimate ordered handoff, yet unguided controls remained more recoverable and economical. Every revised run batch-opened four or five references; two cited probe files absent from the delivered artifact; and some inspected a sound atomic helper without tracing the maker-bound sequence through its caller. The correction therefore targets when context is loaded, where evidence is delivered, and how composed order is verified. A new preference label without those changed behaviors would be a surface win, not a foundational repair.

The first repair cut mean audit input by about 42 percent and made probe evidence recoverable from the delivered report, but references were still loaded too broadly. A stricter one-at-a-time loading sequence then looked cleaner while increasing mean cost and eliminating the ritual-design finding from every replicate. That intervention was rejected. The next change removes duplicated generic testing policy and places the essential authority distinction in the always-loaded kernel. This is why the repository retains failed interventions: they prevent a plausible procedural refinement from being mistaken for a foundational improvement.

That structural change was then falsified against the same exposing boundary. Three fresh auditors all recovered the executable false-pass defects, recognized Relay's genuinely load-bearing commit order, and rejected the unsupported reasoning ritual; their reports retained either direct probe results or sufficient reproductions. They did so while opening fewer generic references and using about 20 percent less mean input than the original candidate round. This does not establish a broad task-value gain, but it supports the causal claim that removing duplicated routes corrected the specific displacement mechanism without repeating the one-reference regression. Independent held-out plugin evidence remains necessary before generalizing the release claim.

Rubric development later supplied a second intervention chain. Removing arbitrary weights and totals did not by itself make the instrument general: the first revised rubric still hard-coded a pairwise `A`/`B` form for a three-candidate decision. Tying the instrument to the declared comparison topology produced a usable three-way rubric, after which independent review could remove residual table and closed-outcome lock without discarding semantic grades, Notes, or free-form reasoning. This is the kind of foundational correction the comparison system is meant to enable: identify the premise that made valid evidence unrepresentable, change that premise, and re-observe the resulting artifact.

The CloseKit exposing round separated discovery quality from finding calibration. Revised audits found materially more of the target's reachable authority and failure-state defects than the older audits, while also promoting some failure-and-retry observations beyond the guarantees the target had adopted. Four of six blind preferences favored the older reports. Independent condition-blind fact checks found those four preferences unsound because they discounted verified High or Medium omissions, but they also confirmed the revised reports' severity overreach. The useful correction was neither shorter audits nor more findings: preserve independently repair-changing discovery and derive each disposition separately from target authority and demonstrated consequence.

The Harborlight round then showed how an apparently correct final choice can coexist with a failed causal test. Every assisted orchestrator repeated the alternatives' intended truthfulness, privacy, and authority behavior in the common worker prompt. The largest run retained seventy-two executions and multiple blind reviews, yet its behavioral comparison had measured the common evaluator guidance as part of every condition. Its final recommendation was rescued by static contract inspection, not by the advertised treatment evidence. The foundational correction therefore belongs in deployed prompt composition and post-composition validation; adding agents, scores, or report polish would only scale the contamination.

After the composed-payload boundary was corrected, the first independent validator made a different category error: it rejected intentionally harmful treatments because they conflicted with the valid public target. Those conflicts were the causal differences the experiment needed to measure. A fresh validator passed after its authority was limited to instrument coherence and observability. This reinforced the same foundation: evaluation controls must preserve the treatment contrast rather than repairing, duplicating, or excluding it before outcomes exist.

The next host-loaded CloseKit intervention removed a named inventory of optional reporters from the always-loaded auditor kernel. Three fresh revised auditors invoked none of those reporters while all three still reproduced the ignored workspace root, confirmation bypass, and path-containment defect. That establishes the specific causal repair: naming every reporter had acted as an execution suite, and removing the affordance eliminated the detour without losing the shared hard findings.

The broader quality claim did not follow. Nine counterbalanced blind judgments preferred the revised audit in all three views of one replicate, the frozen baseline in all three views of another, and split one each among revised, baseline, and no-skill control in the third. Fresh condition-blind fact checks reconciled every decisive premise against the public target and treated the private oracle as a hypothesis. They found a real revised advantage where it uniquely recovered the documented workspace-root failure, a real baseline advantage where it covered more consequential surfaces with better calibration, and no compelled ranking in the split replicate. The foundational correction is therefore not “make reviewers prefer the revision.” It is to make full-audit coverage an observable claim while preserving separate disposition of each finding. Complementary fresh discovery can strengthen that claim when it actually occurred, but mandating an inspector topology later caused three of three agents to report independent work they never performed. Replacing the topology with the outcome removed those false provenance claims without losing the shared hard findings. Without retained coverage evidence, the audit must remain partial even when its report is persuasive.

The paper-game Split Testing round then exposed a different foundational substitution. The treatment created substantially more reviewers, rubric material, and deterministic analysis than the control, but its execution units reviewed rule sheets rather than observing mixed groups learn and play them. One treatment's unanimous inner preference therefore could not establish the maker's usability claim. Another treatment's exhaustive-looking checker omitted East's returned-slip rule, and a third retained a false source hash. Higher-level reviewers often still favored the more elaborate reports. The correction is not a stricter review template: place evidence at the boundary named by the claim and require every derived instrument to remain source-faithful at the scope it asserts.

The held-out recovery-card round then isolated a budget-allocation version of the same error. All three assisted and all three unguided agents reached the correct deterministic choice, but assisted agents repeated the simulator three times per card and sometimes inserted an inner blind panel. Eight of nine outer blind reviewers found the matched reports tied while the assisted runs consumed about 49 percent more input on average. The three-run default had been spent below the actual variable agent boundary, and the inner reviewers duplicated the outer review responsibility. The correction therefore binds repetition to the source of uncertainty and gives every evaluation layer a distinct property; it does not replace the default with a universal smaller number.

The next three-arm cold-storage round showed why removing deterministic repetition alone was not the whole cause. The full revision's inner review rescued a missed case-profile distinction in one replicate and all three outer reviewers preferred that report, but the same revision flattened the distinction and lost unanimously in another replicate. The rescue cost more than three times the old arm's input and wall time in the winning replicate, while all inner reviewers merely restated a frontier whose selection remained the board's value judgment. The foundational correction therefore separates option evidence, artifact QA, and decision authority, and preserves decision-relevant distributions before aggregation. A preference for the rescued report establishes the combined system's output quality in that run; it does not prove the earlier omission was repaired.

The held-out Dispatch comparison then produced an important nonterminal success. Nine of nine blind reviewers preferred the outcome-driven auditor to the prior candidate because it preserved target authority, uncertainty, executable defects, and a smaller self-contained repair boundary. Every reviewer nevertheless identified the same omitted current-host metadata rejection in both arms. Preference established a real report-quality gain, not audit completeness. Adding current primary host authority recovered that defect in two of three fresh runs, but one run launched irrelevant task-value trials and another still treated selected fields as the whole interface. The next repairs therefore remove trial language from routing metadata and bind release evidence to the complete adopted host object. They target the causes exposed by traces rather than adding the missed field to a checklist.

The next attempted coverage repair required a genuinely fresh complementary context with retained launch and return provenance. Two auditors did execute that pass rather than merely claiming it, yet both passes rediscovered the same script and prompt defects and missed the same public-submission boundary. One parent audit imported ambient repository architecture into a copied target, and another probe briefly mutated the user's Codex plugin state before cleanup. Separate generic and Skill-Auditor-assisted coverage critics rejected the resulting “source-complete” claim but still did not recover the decisive publication rule; the assisted critic also escaped its assigned workspace to import parent-repository files. This intervention was rejected. Real independence prevented false provenance but did not manufacture complementary authority or safe behavior. The next correction belongs in focused source ownership, ambient-authority separation, and external-effect boundaries—not in another required reviewer layer.

Splitting host contracts into a focused reference then cut the revised auditor's mean input by about half in a matched Dispatch rerun, but it did not improve the decision artifact reliably. Blind review initially favored the older auditor five to four. Post-reveal fact-checking removed the revision's only unanimous replicate win: its proposed starter repair selected one plausible target meaning as though it were maker authority, and reviewers incorrectly treated a declared MIT contract as mere packaging preference. Another replicate favored the older audit because it retained adjacent passing behavior that better bounded the repair. The reference split is therefore useful context discipline, not a quality result. The next correction targets repair authority, scoped contract incorporation, and preservation evidence; adding another discovery agent or report section would miss the cause.

The subsequent Driftlight trial supplied a positive result on a dissimilar open-ended decision: nine of nine counterbalanced blind reviews preferred the current Split Testing-assisted artifacts, and independent raw-data fact-checks preserved all three comparative directions. The reasons were specific—valid unpaired inference, stronger custody and reproducibility, and disclosure of burden, eventfulness, and subgroup counterevidence—not generic process polish. The fact-checks also corrected material treatment claims, so preference did not close the decision. Assisted executions used about 2.9 times the mean input and 2.1 times the mean wall time of controls; the most elaborate assisted run spent heavily on an inner panel that improved artifact QA without adding option evidence or decision authority. The supported foundation is therefore narrower than “more evaluation improves quality”: preserve the comparative evidence that changed the reports, attribute every rescue and cost to its condition, and remove layers that do not observe a distinct unsettled property.

The eDNA follow-up then tested whether one more semantic sentence would repair a repeatedly missed composed-authority boundary. The new sentence named only the general relationship between individually valid roles and their runtime composition; worker prompts still contained no defect hint. One of three fresh audits found destructive output/input identity collapse, while two repeated the verified omission. Mean input and wall time both rose by about 18 percent. The intervention was reverted before blind grading because its deterministic success condition had already failed. This is direct evidence that a plausible foundational explanation is not yet a foundational repair; adding language must change behavior reliably enough to justify its attention cost.

A second eDNA intervention targeted the audit's release gate rather than the missed identity concept. It required fresh falsification before claiming release readiness or a sufficient repair. Three fresh auditors responded by recommending such a challenge after repair, but none executed and retained it in the current audit, none recovered the known identity defect, and one lost a previously consistent no-clobber finding. The change was also reverted. Deferring verification can make prose more cautious without improving the evidence available now; an instruction that names a future gate is not the execution of that gate.

## Consequences

Reports preserve the causal support and competing explanations needed to act, not only winners and scores. Follow-up work targets a decision-changing uncertainty or foundational correction; it does not add tests merely to make the evidence package look comprehensive. A correction remains provisional when the causal link is not observed.

Reopen the direction when a new task population, model, host, condition, trace, counterexample, or measurement defect breaks the claimed causal chain.
