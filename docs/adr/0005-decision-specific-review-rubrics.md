# ADR 0005: Use a shared, researched rubric for each blind comparison

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-15

## Decision

Every semantic comparison has an explicit rubric created for the user's actual decision. It honors maker-supplied criteria and applicable authority, researches relevant current rubrics, standards, and domain practice broadly enough to avoid a consequential omission, and adds task-specific judgment where existing sources are insufficient.

The rubric is frozen without candidate outputs, condition identities, or a desired winner. Every reviewer uses the same core rubric to grade final outputs. Specialist perspectives may supplement the core while remaining separately attributed. The repository does not ship a universal rubric, category inventory, scale, weight scheme, or report template.

Every retained semantic criterion has shared, decision-specific grade semantics; the scale may be categorical, ordinal, quantitative, or mixed, and preserves not-assessable evidence. A trusted executable fact need not be redundantly model-scored. Every semantic grade retains reviewer-written Notes tied to observable evidence. Each review also retains a substantial free-form comparative assessment as its primary rationale. Grades aid comparison; they do not replace the explanation, counterevidence, uncertainty, or material concerns outside anticipated rubric items. This asks for an auditable decision rationale, not private chain-of-thought.

The rubric is a measurement model, not a reviewer-output template. It contains only separately useful dimensions whose treatment can change the decision. Worksheets, fixed headings, minimum word counts, closed verdict vocabularies, pass bands, and research-derived concern inventories require a real maker or external interface; absent that authority, they are template lock rather than rigor.

Mechanically decidable rubric relations—such as weight totals, score ranges, caps, and eligibility logic—receive executable or direct arithmetic validation before candidate exposure. Semantic review owns judgment about whether the instrument measures the decision; it is not evidence that the instrument's arithmetic is coherent.

The rubric, public task, supplied source, controlling authority, checker or reference, and any private expected observations must describe the same problem. Private evaluator intent cannot override a worker-visible maker contract. Retained numerical or mandatory structure also requires a decision-grounded justification; arithmetic consistency alone does not supply one.

## Why

Leaving standards implicit makes reviewer agreement difficult to interpret: reviewers can reach different conclusions because they valued different outcomes rather than because they observed different evidence. A universal rubric fails in the opposite direction; the properties that matter for code, a game, a video, a safety decision, and an instruction system are not interchangeable.

A fresh blind rubric-author trial supplied additional outcome evidence. Given a task-specific public plugin target and an explicit warning against a universal template, the author still produced nine mandatory categories, a worksheet, fixed verdict labels, numeric bands, and an arbitrary 600-word minimum. The domain sources improved coverage, but the visible ceremony would have rewarded rubric-shaped audits and increased reviewer burden. The instrument was rejected before candidate exposure and motivated the boundary above.

Research reduces the chance that a controller invents a weaker substitute for an established domain standard. Custom synthesis remains necessary because a familiar rubric may serve a different population or decision. Blinding the rubric author from outputs prevents criteria from being chosen to favor the artifact already seen.

In a later fresh rubric trial, an author labeled a rubric as 100 points while defining sections that could award 110. A separate semantic validity reviewer initially described that scoring structure as aligned. Direct arithmetic exposed the contradiction before any candidate output was generated. The failure was in the measurement instrument and its verification boundary, not in reviewer effort or report wording.

A subsequent dual-host audit instrument repeated the deeper failure after the arithmetic lessons had already been written down. Its public brief and rubric implied that both capabilities were required on both hosts, while the target's controlling instruction explicitly allowed a host to expose either capability or both. The rubric also reintroduced unsupported pass bands and fixed severity vocabulary. Nine fresh audits predictably promoted the manufactured capability gap into a finding. The round was rejected before grading. Executable fixture validation had proved that the task ran; it had not proved that the task, oracle, rubric, and target authority meant the same thing.

A fresh high-effort Luna rubric author later reproduced template lock on a non-software safety-video decision despite an explicit instruction not to reward experiment ceremony. It invented four gates, six weighted categories, a 65-point floor, an 8-point winning margin, unanimity logic, and a mandatory review record without maker evidence for those exact values. A separate medium-effort Luna validator found two genuine scope gaps but endorsed the invented arithmetic by supplying a plausible purpose after the fact. This showed that research breadth, model strength, and independent semantic review do not create authority for a measurement structure. Exact numbers and mandatory forms need an independently established decision basis; a fluent post-hoc rationale is not that basis.

A later matched intervention on one three-candidate audit decision separated two more causes. The unguided rubric author invented weights, a total, and a fixed record form, and its independent validator again rationalized them after the fact. The Split Testing-assisted author removed the unsupported arithmetic but still froze the instrument around `A` and `B`, so it could not represent the declared three-way comparison. A narrow instruction change tied the rubric to the actual candidate grouping and cardinality; a fresh author then produced a three-candidate instrument. An independent validator removed its remaining mandatory table and closed outcome fields while preserving common semantic grades, evidence-grounded Notes, and substantial free-form comparison. The improvement came from correcting measurement authority and topology, not from adding a more elaborate rubric template.

A fresh Harborlight rubric author then produced a substantively strong six-criterion instrument with shared grade meanings, per-item Notes, free-form comparison, ties, and no aggregate score. A separate high-effort validator, still isolated from candidate outputs, found two decision-changing omissions: the public no-account-or-transaction-tools constraint was not explicit in the rubric, and its comparison criterion did not unambiguously require evidence across all four immutable proposals. It also made unanticipated issues explicitly representable. The correction changed the measurement contract before review without inventing a universal category or arithmetic scheme. This supplies local evidence that research and a strong rubric author improve an instrument but do not replace an independent semantic coherence check against the exact public task.

A later CloseKit listwise instrument was strong on the same semantic properties but still failed its first pre-exposure validation. One sentence prohibited reviewers from inspecting "any candidate output," while the rest of the rubric required them to inspect and compare the three candidate audit artifacts. The same validator also found that rejecting arithmetic winners was insufficient without a qualitative winner/tie boundary. Both defects were corrected before candidate exposure, and a fresh validator then accepted the instrument. This was not report polish: the original instruction made the intended observation internally impossible and left close cases vulnerable to an implicit score count.

The held-out recovery-card comparison then showed that requiring a "decision-grounded basis" was still too easy to satisfy rhetorically. A fresh high-effort author, isolated from candidate outputs, invented 40/20/20/10/10 weights, traffic-light bands, deterministic tie-breaks, and a mandatory worksheet. It justified every choice in fluent prose, but the maker supplied no calibrated exchange rate, loss function, or literal review interface. The instrument was rejected before exposure. The reusable correction is sharper: numeric tradeoffs need maker-fixed loss, empirical calibration, or a formal consequence model, and required information does not imply required layout. A post-hoc story about why invented numbers seem sensible is not measurement evidence.

The held-out Driftlight game comparison reproduced the same failure before candidate exposure in a different domain. Its first otherwise thoughtful rubric prescribed ten mandatory items, a four-part Note worksheet, and fixed outcome labels. A revised instrument retained five separately decision-changing lenses, shared qualitative grade meanings, evidence-grounded Notes, and substantial free-form comparison while removing totals, fixed verdicts, and literal response shape. A fresh validator accepted the revised instrument. Nine later blind reviews used it to surface different material tradeoffs rather than converging on rubric-shaped prose, and three fact-checks corrected both preferred and non-preferred artifacts without needing a new rubric category. This is local evidence that common decision meaning and open rationale can preserve discrimination while avoiding template lock; it is not evidence that five lenses or that domain's grade scale should be reused.

The next held-out eDNA Skill Auditor comparison exposed a deeper rubric-authority failure. A fresh author and fresh validator both approved an instrument that made the repository's instruction-design lens a decisive target anchor. Three blind reviewers then preferred the assisted audit, largely because it recommended relaxing unsupported TRACE and note choreography. Condition-blind fact-checkers preserved that recommendation as useful design judgment but found that the target had not adopted the lens as a conformance rule. Two of three preferences reversed after harder target omissions—default no-clobber behavior and destructive input/output identity collapse—were restored to their proper force. A criterion therefore retains both its source and its authority level: a house design preference may judge usefulness without becoming target truth, and independent validation cannot manufacture the missing authority.

A prospective replacement instrument then made criterion authority and decision force explicit without adding scores or a fixed report form. An independent validator accepted the exact deployed rubric before revised audits existed. Three blind reviewers preferred the revised audits, but condition-blind fact-checking reversed one preference because its revised audit omitted a hard `collector` output-type violation found by the comparator. The instrument correction therefore worked without turning the rubric into truth: stronger authority calibration improved the reviews, while post-judgment factual reconciliation still remained necessary.

A later Dispatch audit instrument showed that removing arithmetic alone does not remove template lock. A fresh author and an independent validator both preserved mandatory sections, fixed finding ingredients, and a closed vocabulary even though the decision required only common semantic meaning and inspectable support. A fresh open-form revision removed the literal report form while preserving the same material distinctions and verification responsibilities. The useful contract was what each reviewer had to be able to judge, not how every judgment had to look.

That Dispatch instrument then exposed an authority failure in the validation layer itself. Its first independent validator imported the parent repository's governing rules into the rubric even though the audited executors received only the common request, their local workspace instruction, and the target. A fresh correction context given that exact deployed authority stack removed the absent requirements while preserving the target-derived operational distinctions. Independence had reduced author error but had not supplied criterion authority; without input-stack reconciliation it made the foreign rules look better founded.

Recent external measurements point in the same direction without substituting for the local result. [PReMISE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30803v1) found that high agreement did not imply low rubric exploitability and that no tested source dominated reliability, preference fit, structural adequacy, and adversarial robustness. [RuVerBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29920v1) found substantial noise even in frontier models verifying rubrics over agentic outputs, with diminishing returns from majority voting. A researched rubric and a strong judge therefore remain instruments whose behavior must be validated against the decision, not authorities created by fluency or consensus.

## Consequences

Retain the exact rubric and its consequential source/version basis with prompts, artifacts, checks, per-item Notes, free-form assessments, and judgments. A rubric may be qualitative, quantitative, or mixed; it succeeds by discriminating material outcomes, not by producing a number. A hard eligibility or correctness failure can outweigh an aggregate grade.

Reopen the rubric when the objective, audience, governing standard, domain evidence, or output type changes. If a frozen rubric proves unable to distinguish a plausible material failure, preserve that as a measurement defect and run a visibly new review rather than silently editing prior grades.
